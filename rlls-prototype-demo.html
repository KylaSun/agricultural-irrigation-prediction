RLLS · research prototype

Low-cost, soil-sensor-confirmed, weather-aware irrigation decision support. RLLS is designed to augment farmer judgement with transparent recommendations.

Zone 1 · advisory mode
Research position. This prototype demonstrates a decision method and historical sensor replay. It does not claim measured water savings, autonomous valve control, or field deployment.

2 · Transparent decision method

Sector-specific thresholds must be calibrated locally
Sense

Read current root-zone soil moisture. Values outside 0–100% become a sensor-check state.

Confirm

Below 40% enters irrigation logic; 40–60% remains in the operating band; ≥60% prevents irrigation.

Anticipate

Forecast rain ≥5 mm changes a dry-soil action from irrigate to wait and recheck.

Protect

The system explains every decision and keeps a human operator responsible for final actuation.

5 · Robustness experiment: controlled missingness

Random masking sensitivity test

What was tested

We progressively hid 0%, 10%, 30%, 50%, and 70% of sensor readings, then re-ran the same prediction task. This models information loss under controlled conditions.

What happened

Soil-only was strongest when data were complete (F1 0.478), fell sharply at 50% masking (0.110), and recovered only partially at 70% (0.199). Results across the richer configurations also varied and remained low.

Interpretation: availability and missing-data handling are part of system performance. The curve is a case-study stress test, not evidence that sensors in China or elsewhere have the same reliability.

7 · RLLS answer and next study

Evidence-led conclusion

Current advisory method

Current soil moisture → threshold rule → recommendation.

Next research model

Current soil moisture + weather + past irrigation → predict 24-hour soil moisture → predict threshold crossing → recommendation.

RLLS answer: begin with a calibrated soil-moisture signal, measure whether extra sensing improves dry-event detection, and make missingness visible before trusting automation. The next study should use exact-clock targets, rolling temporal validation, realistic contiguous outages, local crop/soil calibration, and external farms. Do not use future irrigation volume as an input, because it would leak future information.

Research context

Design inspiration, not claims of novelty

Existing irrigation DSS research shows the value of combining soil-water status with forecast information and farmer-facing guidance. RLLS contributes a bounded low-cost, transparent prototype using the project’s real sensor data. See WIDSSLI, soil-moisture forecasting for irrigation recommendation, and a low-cost IoT olive-grove DSS.
